I spent the last week of January guesting at Bunker Ink in Tirana — five days,
eleven finished pieces, one wrist that needed two evenings of ice. It was my
first time working in Albania since I started tattooing, and half of my books
filled within a day of the announcement, mostly with spine and sternum
projects.

Working away from your own studio is a stress test for your process. My
stencils, my needle groupings, my healing sheets all travel. What doesn't
travel is the light — every studio's light lies differently, and dotwork lives
and dies by reading skin density correctly. Day one I packed dots too tight on
a client with much drier skin than I'm used to; I caught it early, opened the
spacing, and made a note that is now taped above my station in Skopje.

## The pieces

The week's best work was a sternum rosette for a violinist — twelve petals,
dot-gradient shading, built around a scar she stopped hiding that day. Healed
photos in six months, if she agrees.

We're planning the return visit already: two artists from Bunker Ink guest at
Crna Reka this autumn. Dates go in the journal and on Instagram first.
