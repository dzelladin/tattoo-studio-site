The most common misunderstanding about heavy black: people think of it as
filling, like painting a wall. It is closer to carpentry. You are building a
surface that has to stay flat — visually flat — on a material that swells,
sheds and scars, and every shortcut shows forever.

A solid black forearm at this studio is always two sessions, four to six weeks
apart. The first pass saturates; then the skin does its work — it lifts what
it will not hold, unevenly, in ways you cannot fully predict. The second pass
reads that map and corrects it. One-session blackout exists, but you will meet
its patchiness at month three, and fixing it costs more than doing it right.

## The question to ask

Before committing to blackout, ask your artist: *what is your plan for the
edges?* A black surface is only as good as its border — where it meets bare
skin, wraps a wrist bone, or fades into dotwork. If the answer is a shrug, the
middle will not save you. Edges are drawn on the standing, moving body, on
tattoo day, never on a tablet the night before.

Blackout is also the most honest work we sell: no motif, no story to hide
behind. Just a decision, held evenly, across a lot of skin.
