Every few weeks someone brings us a screenshot of an "ethno" tattoo — a rosette
from a tourist brochure, red-and-black, framed like a fridge magnet — and asks
for the same. We usually say no, and we owe a public explanation of why.

A motif from a Mariovo apron was not decoration. It was a system: the diamond
held a threat out, the wheel held a family line together, the placement on the
cloth mirrored a placement in life — hem, chest, shoulder. When you lift the
motif off the cloth and drop it on skin *without carrying the system*, what you
get is a souvenir. Kitsch is not a style; it is a shortcut.

## What we do instead

We start from documentation, not from graphics. Ethnographic archives, museum
plates, photographs of originals — then we redraw the geometry from scratch for
the specific body. The eight-fold wheel a weaver built for a flat panel has to
be rebuilt to breathe around a forearm that twists. That redrawing is the work.
It is slower, it costs more, and it is the only version of "traditional" we are
willing to sign.

If your grandmother's dowry chest has a carving you want to carry, bring a
photograph of the chest — not a vector someone traced from it. The difference
shows up on skin, and it shows up in ten years.
